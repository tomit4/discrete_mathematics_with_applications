20. Refer to Example 9.1.3. Suppose you are appearing on a game show with a
    prize behind one of five closed doors: $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$, and $E$. If you
    pick the correct door, you win the prize. You pick door $A$. The game show
    host then opens one of the other doors and reveals that there is no prize
    behind it. Then the host gives you the option of staying with your original
    choice of door $A$ or switching to one of the other doors that is still
    closed.

a. If you stick with your original choice, what is the probability that you will
win the prize?

The prize is behind $1$ of the $5$ doors. After the host reveals that the prize
is not behind $1$ of the $5$ doors, the prize is behind $1$ of the $4$ remaining
doors.

As I chose door $A$ before the big reveal, my chances of being correct in
sticking with my original choice remains fixed to that original probability,
that of $\dfrac{1}{5}$, or $20\%$.

b. If you switch to another door, what is the probability that you will win the
prize?

When the host reveals that one of the doors does not have the prize, the
remaining $\dfrac{4}{5}$ probability is distributed to the remaining three other
doors:

$$ \frac{4}{5} \cdot \frac{1}{3} = \frac{4}{15} \approx 26.7\% $$

a. How many positive two-digit integers are multiples of $3$?

$$ S = \{12, 15, 18, 21, 24, 27, 30, 33, 36, 39, 42, 45, 48, 51, 54, 57, 60, 63, 66, 69, 72, 75, 78, 81, 84, 87, 90, 93, 96, 99\} $$

Note that:

$$ 12 = 3 \cdot 4, 15 = 3 \cdot 5, \dots, 99 = 3 \cdot 33 $$

So, the sample space is as large as the number of integers from $4$ to $33$

By Theorem 9.1.1, if $m, n \in \mathbb{Z}$, and $m \leq n$, then there are
$n - m + 1$ integers from $m$ to $n$ inclusive. Therefore, let $m = 4$, and
$n = 33$:

$$ 33 - 4 + 1 = 30 $$

And thus there are $30$ positive two-digit integers that are multiples of $3$.

b. What is the probability that a randomly chosen positive two-digit integer is
a multiple of $3$?

The amount of positive two-digit integers goes from $10$ to $99$, so the amount
of 2 digit integers is:

$$ 99 - 10 + 1 = 90 $$

This is the total sample space in this case. Now, the total event space, $E$,
(that that two-digit integer is a multiple of $3$), is $30$, so:

$$ P(E) =\frac{30}{90} = \frac{1}{3} = 33 \frac{1}{3}\%  $$

b. What is the probability that a randomly chosen positive two-digit integer is
a multiple of $4$?

$$ S = \{12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32, 36, 40, 44, 48, 52, 56, 60, 64, 68, 72, 76, 80, 84, 88, 92, 96\} $$

Now:

$$ 12 = 3 \cdot 4, 16 = 4 \cdot 4, 20 = 5 \cdot 4, \dots 96 = 24 \cdot 4 $$

So, let $m = 3$, and $n = 24$:

$$ 24 - 3 + 1 = 22 $$

Then, the probability of this event, $E$, is:

$$ P(E) = \frac{22}{90} \approx 24.4\%  $$

24. Suppose $A[1], A[2], \dots, A[n]$ is a one-dimensional array and $n \geq 2$.
    Consider the subarray

$$ A[1], A[2], \dots, A\left[\lfloor \frac{n}{2} \rfloor\right] $$

a. How many elements are in the sub-array

    i. if $n$ is even?

If $n$ is even, then $\left\lfloor \dfrac{n}{2} \right\rfloor = \dfrac{n}{2}$,
so there are $\dfrac{n}{2}$ elements in the sub-array.

    ii. if $n$ is odd?

If $n$ is odd, then
$\left\lfloor \dfrac{n}{2} \right\rfloor = \dfrac{n - 1}{2}$, so there are
$\dfrac{n - 1}{2}$ elements in the sub-array.

b. What is the probability that a randomly chosen array element is in the
sub-array

    i. if $n$ is even?

$$ P(E) = \dfrac{\dfrac{n}{2}}{n} = \frac{n}{2n} =\frac{1}{2} = 50\%  $$

    ii. if $n$ is odd?

$$ P(E) = \dfrac{\dfrac{n - 1}{2}}{n} = \frac{n - 1}{2n} $$

Note that for odd, this means that $P(E)$ approaches $50\%$ as $n \to \infty$.

25. Suppose $A[1], A[2], \dots, A[n]$ is a one-dimensional array and $n \geq 2$.
    Consider the subarray

$$ A\left[\lfloor \frac{n}{2} \rfloor\right], A\left[\lfloor \frac{n}{2} \rfloor + 1\right], \dots, A[n] $$

a. How many elements are in the sub-array

    i. if $n$ is even?

If $n$ is even, then $\left \lfloor \dfrac{n}{2} \right\rfloor = \dfrac{n}{2}$.

$$ n - \frac{n}{2} + 1 = \frac{n}{2} + 1 $$

So there are $\dfrac{n}{2} + 1$ elements in the sub-array if $n$ is even.

    ii. if $n$ is odd?

If $n$ is odd, then
$\left\lfloor \dfrac{n}{2} \right\rfloor = \dfrac{n - 1}{2}$.

$$ n - \frac{n - 1}{2} + 1 = \frac{2n - (n - 1)}{2} + 1 = \frac{n + 1}{2} + 1 = \frac{n + 1 + 2}{2} = \frac{n + 3}{2} $$

So, there are $\dfrac{n + 3}{2}$ elements in the sub-array when $n$ is odd.

b. What is the probability that a randomly chosen array element is in the
sub-array

    i. if $n$ is even?

$$ P(E) = \frac{\dfrac{n}{2} + 1}{n}$$

$$ = \frac{\dfrac{n + 2}{2}}{n}$$

$$ = \frac{n + 2}{2n} $$

    ii. if $n$ is odd?

$$ P(E) = \frac{\frac{n + 3}{2}}{n} $$

$$ = \frac{n + 3}{2n} $$

So, the smallest integer in a set of 56 consecutive integers with the largest
